**A recycled PET acoustic panel is a dense felt board pressed from used drinks bottles. It soaks up speech (the 12 mm rPET Panel reaches αw 0.80, class B to ISO 11654, on a 100 mm air cavity and αw 1.00, class A, over a 50 mm cavity filled with 50 mm stone wool), cuts and paints like a joinery board, takes UV print and goes up with clips or adhesive, at a price per square metre below veneered wood.** What follows is the fact sheet of our rPET range, from raw material to the comparison with wood.

## What is a recycled PET acoustic panel?

PET is the plastic of water and soft-drink bottles. Once collected, the bottles are sorted, washed and shredded; the flakes are melted into fibre and pressed under heat into a dense board, and that board is PET felt. Our [rPET range](/en/products/pet-acoustic-panels) is made entirely from recycled PET, and the fibre does three jobs at once: it carries itself, it is the visible face and it is the absorber. No fabric, no veneer.

Re-Sound makes two families from it. [rPET Groove](/en/products/rpet-groove) is the grooved version, cut from the sheet: ten stock colours and any other colour to order, widths of 600 and 1,200 mm, thicknesses of 12, 24 and 36 mm. [rPET Panel](/en/products/rpet-panel) is the plain, solid sheet in 9 and 12 mm, in formats of 2,800 × 1,220 and 2,440 × 1,220 mm; how much it absorbs depends on how you mount it, from αw 0.25 (class D) straight onto the wall up to 1.00 (class A) over a filled 50 mm cavity (measured on the 12 mm sheet to EN ISO 354). Where a wall curves or a column needs wrapping, [rPET Flex Groove](/en/products/rpet-flex-groove) bends to a minimum radius of 500 mm thanks to its V-cut grooves.

## How PET felt absorbs sound

Sound enters the felt, sets the fibres moving and loses its energy there as friction. The entire panel takes part, edges and back included. The measured figures for the rPET range:

| Product and mounting | αw (ISO 11654) | Class | NRC (ASTM C423) |
|---|---|---|---|
| rPET Panel 12 mm, fixed directly to the wall | 0.25 | D | 0.40 |
| rPET Panel 12 mm, 50 mm air cavity | 0.60 | C | 0.75 |
| rPET Panel 12 mm, 100 mm air cavity | 0.80 | B | 0.80 |
| rPET Panel 12 mm, 50 mm cavity filled with 50 mm stone wool | 1.00 | A | 0.95 |
| rPET Panel 12 mm, 100 mm cavity filled with 100 mm stone wool | 0.95 | A | 0.95 |
| rPET Groove, 12 / 24 / 36 mm | not published | – | 0.55 / 0.75 / 0.90 |

The 9 mm sheet has not been measured yet, so each figure belongs to the 12 mm sheet with the mounting named beside it; and a cavity behind the panel, filled or not, does more for the low frequencies than the thickness of the felt.

Class A (αw 0.90 or more) is within reach with the rPET Panel over a filled 50 mm cavity (αw 1.00), or with our textile range [Interior](/en/products/interior) (αw 1.0). In most offices, schools and restaurants class B is plenty; area, mounting and placement matter more than the step from 0.80 to 1.00. The reverberation time a room should reach depends on its use: ISO 22955 for open-plan offices and, for UK projects, BB93 for schools and BS 8233 for buildings in general. Check the exact requirement with your acoustician.

## How many m² do you need? A Sabine example

Take a classroom of 8 × 7 m with a 3.2 m ceiling, so **179 m³**, with a tiled floor, a plasterboard ceiling and one glazed wall. Measured reverberation time: **1.2 s**. As a reference for the target we borrow the method of the German standard DIN 18041, which puts teaching and meeting rooms in its group A3 (teaching/communication) and sets T = 0.32 · lg(V) − 0.17 s:

T = 0.32 × lg(179) − 0.17 = 0.32 × 2.25 − 0.17 = 0.72 − 0.17 = **0.55 s**

BB93 sets its own limits for UK schools; check the exact requirement with your acoustician. Sabine's formula, **A = 0.161 × V / T**, then tells you how much absorption to add:

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption in the room today | 0.161 × 179 ÷ 1.2 | 24.0 m² Sabine |
| Absorption at the target | 0.161 × 179 ÷ 0.55 | 52.4 m² Sabine |
| To add | 52.4 − 24.0 | 28.4 m² Sabine |

Solving that entirely with the 12 mm rPET Panel on a 100 mm air cavity (αw 0.80, class B) would take 28.4 ÷ 0.80 = **35.5 m² of panel**: eleven sheets of 2,800 × 1,220 mm (11 × 3.42 = 37.6 m²). A combination is more practical. **30 m² of class A ceiling** (assume αw 0.9, for instance an [acoustic stretch ceiling](https://stretchplafond.be/products/acoustic-stretch-system) from our sister company STRETCH) delivers 30 × 0.9 = 27.0 m² Sabine, and **two rPET Panels** on a 100 mm air cavity on the back wall add 2 × 3.42 = 6.84 m² × 0.80 = 5.5 m² Sabine. Together 32.5 m² Sabine, more than the 28.4 required, and the room lands at 0.161 × 179 ÷ (24.0 + 32.5) = **0.51 s**.

Panels take the reverberation out of a room; they do not stop the phone call carrying across an open-plan floor. ## Fire safety and emissions: B-s1,d0 and OEKO-TEX

In schools and public buildings the reaction-to-fire class to EN 13501-1 is usually a hard requirement. For the rPET range the classification follows the colour: white, grey and black are **B-s1,d0** (limited contribution to fire, little smoke, s1, no flaming droplets, d0); every other colour is **B-s2,d0**. Standard and FR versions are classified alike, and classification reports are available on request. Which class your project must meet depends on the building and its use; check the exact requirement with your acoustician and your fire engineer.

For indoor air, the rPET felt is certified to **OEKO-TEX® Standard 100**, the test scheme that screens textiles for harmful substances.

## Cutting, painting, UV printing and fixing

On site an rPET panel cuts **with a knife, a circular saw, a panel saw or an angle grinder with a metal-cutting blade**; a joiner builds it into cupboards, counters or partitions and trims cut-outs on the spot, and a CNC router takes shapes, cut-outs and V-cuts for folded corners out of it.

The felt can be **painted**, with water-based or solvent-based paint, when you need an exact house colour. And Re-Sound **UV-prints rPET panels** in-house: logos, wayfinding, artwork, quoted per job.

Two ways to **fix** it:

- **With clips**: the panel comes off again and can be reused, ideal for rented offices and for take-back later.
- **Glued**: for a permanent installation on a flat, clean, load-bearing substrate. Screws or battens work too.

At the end of its life the take-back is free in five countries: Belgium, the Netherlands, France, Germany and Luxembourg. It does not apply outside those five. Read more under [sustainability](/en/sustainability).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a recycled PET panel absorb as well as a wood panel?**
Yes, provided the mounting is right. The 12 mm rPET Panel reaches αw 0.80 (class B to ISO 11654) on a 100 mm air cavity and αw 1.00 (class A) over a 50 mm cavity filled with 50 mm stone wool; rPET Groove is rated by NRC, 0.55 / 0.75 / 0.90 at 12 / 24 / 36 mm. rWood Groove and rWood Micro both sit at αw 0.90 (class A), Micro rising to 1.00 with 50 mm mineral wool. The difference lies in build-up, look, budget and lead time, not in the material.

**Which thickness should I choose: 12, 24 or 36 mm?**
For rPET Groove the absorption climbs with thickness: NRC 0.55 at 12 mm, 0.75 at 24 mm and 0.90 at 36 mm. For the plain 12 mm rPET Panel the mounting decides, from αw 0.25 (class D) straight onto the wall to 1.00 (class A) over a filled 50 mm cavity. A cavity behind the panel helps in the low frequencies; 12 mm is the choice where space is tight or the panel is built into furniture. Which reverberation time your room must reach is a question for your acoustician.

**How are PET panels fixed, and can they be printed?**
With clips (demountable, handy in rented premises) or glued for a permanent installation. On site you cut the felt with a knife, a circular saw, a panel saw or an angle grinder; it can be painted, and Re-Sound UV-prints rPET panels in-house, so logos, wayfinding and artwork are all possible. Delivery takes about 3–4 working weeks.
