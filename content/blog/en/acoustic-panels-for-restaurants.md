**In a restaurant with a reverberation time above 1 second, guests talk louder, stay shorter and mention it in their review.** Acoustic panels on the ceiling and on the walls behind the banquettes bring the reverberation back to 0.6–0.8 seconds: conversations stay at the table, the atmosphere stays lively, and the room sounds full without being loud. This article explains which reverberation time suits which concept, where panels pay back most, what the fire class means for hospitality and what a 100 m² dining room needs.

## The Lombard effect: why your restaurant makes itself louder

Hard floors, glass, an open kitchen and a concrete ceiling bounce every conversation around the room several times. Whoever can no longer hear their dinner companion speaks up; the next table does the same. That escalation, the Lombard effect, is why a room with forty guests on a Friday night sounds twice as loud as with twenty. It is also why "too loud" turns up so often in restaurant reviews, and why guests skip dessert and coffee after the main course. Absorption breaks the spiral: less reverberation, a lower background level, and guests talking at a normal volume again.

## Which reverberation time suits which concept

There is no legal requirement for the reverberation time of a restaurant, but there is a clear practice:

| Concept | Target |
|---|---|
| Fine dining, hotel restaurant | 0.5–0.6 s |
| Brasserie, bistro | 0.6–0.8 s |
| Bar, food hall, function room | 0.8–1.0 s (lively, yet intelligible at the table) |

An untreated room with tiles and glass often measures 1.2 to 1.6 s when empty. Guests bring absorption of their own (allow 0.4 to 0.5 m² Sabine per person), so a room that reaches 0.8 s empty sits around 0.6 s when full. Design for the empty room; the full room then takes care of itself.

## Ceiling first

The ceiling is the largest surface and the only one with no table, door or artwork in the way. Two routes:

- **Acoustic stretch ceiling**: a perforated membrane with absorption behind it, seamless up to 5 m wide, in matt colours or printed, fitted in one day without dust, ideal for a refit between two services. Our sister company STRETCH installs them; read more at [stretchplafond.be](https://stretchplafond.be/products/acoustic-stretch-system).
- **Ceiling panels or rafts** in recycled PET where the ceiling has to stay visible, for example above an open kitchen or under a historic structure.

Rule of thumb: making 50 to 70% of the ceiling absorbent solves most of the problem; the walls do the rest.

## Fire safety and upkeep in hospitality

Hospitality falls under strict fire rules, and the fire officer looks at wall and ceiling linings.

- **Fire class (EN 13501-1)**: the rPET range (Panel, Groove, Flex Groove) is B-s1,d0 in white, grey and black and B-s2,d0 in the other colours (limited combustibility, little smoke, no flaming droplets); the rWood range (Groove, Micro, Perf) is B-s1,d0 on the fire-retardant MDF core. Always ask for the classification report and keep it in your fire safety file; we supply it on request. Check the exact requirement with your acoustician and your fire officer.
- **Grease and moisture**: no absorbent panel belongs directly above a fryer or grill. Above the open kitchen an acoustic stretch ceiling at a distance works better than panels close by.
- **Cleaning**: PET felt is vacuumed and spot-treated; wood is treated like a piece of furniture; the Interior covers go in the washing machine. A damaged panel is replaced individually.
- **Emissions**: rPET is OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 certified.

## Worked example for 100 m²

Dining room of 12.5 × 8 m, 3.2 m high (320 m³), tiled floor, lots of glass, 50 covers. Measured empty: **1.4 s**. Target: **0.7 s** (brasserie).

Sabine's formula, A = 0.161 × V / T, gives:

- absorption present: 0.161 × 320 / 1.4 = **36.8 m² Sabine**
- absorption at the target: 0.161 × 320 / 0.7 = **73.6 m² Sabine**
- to add: **36.8 m² Sabine**

Proposal: **35 m² of ceiling** in class A (αw 0.9, so 31.5 m² Sabine) plus **8 m² of rWood Micro** behind the banquettes (at αw 0.90 about 7.2 m² Sabine) = 38.7 m² Sabine. Fully booked (50 guests, roughly 22 m² Sabine extra) the room drops to about 0.5 s: fine-dining level during service, lively enough at aperitif time. The method is set out step by step in [How many acoustic panels do I need?](/en/blog/how-many-acoustic-panels-do-i-need).

## Phasing and budget

Not every business can close for a week. A phasing that works:

1. **Measure** (half an hour, on a quiet morning) and tackle the ceiling: the biggest effect, in one day with a stretch ceiling.
2. **The wall behind the banquettes** and the wall facing the bar: 8–15 m² of rWood or rPET, fitted on a closing day.
3. **Divide modules** at the big tables as the finishing touch, with no installation.
