**In a classroom the reverberation time decides whether pupils understand the teacher and whether the teacher still has a voice at four o'clock.** The Belgian standard NBN S 01-400-2 sets maximum reverberation times for school buildings; for an ordinary classroom that comes down in practice to roughly 0.6 to 0.8 seconds, a common order of magnitude across Europe, while an untreated room with plasterboard, glass and a tiled floor often sits above 1 second. In the UK, BB93 sets its own limits per room type. This article explains what the standards ask, how to measure, how much absorption you need and which solutions last ten school years.

## Why reverberation in a classroom costs more than you think

Speech intelligibility drops quickly once the reverberation goes past 0.8 s: the teacher's syllables run into each other, especially at the back of the room and for pupils who speak another language at home or hear less well. The teacher compensates by speaking louder (the Lombard effect), the pupils do the same, and the room gets louder still. The outcome is familiar from research into voice complaints among teachers and from complaints about concentration in open learning environments. The remedy costs less than the complaints: add absorption on the ceiling and on the back wall.

## The standards: NBN S 01-400-2 in Belgium, BB93 in the UK

NBN S 01-400-2 sets the acoustic criteria for school buildings in Belgium. It covers airborne sound insulation between rooms, impact sound, noise from building services and the **reverberation time** per room type. For reverberation the standard works like this:

- The requirement is a **maximum nominal reverberation time** that depends on room type and volume; it is assessed in the furnished, unoccupied room.
- There are two levels: **normal acoustic comfort** (the basic requirement) and **enhanced acoustic comfort** (stricter, advisable for pupils with hearing or language difficulties).
- Besides classrooms there are separate requirements for, among others, dining halls, sports halls, corridors and assembly halls, the rooms where reverberation is traditionally highest.

For a standard classroom of 180 to 250 m³ the Belgian requirement lands in practice between 0.6 and 0.8 s, and that range is a common order of magnitude elsewhere too. By way of comparison, the method of the German standard DIN 18041 sets T = 0.32·lg(V) − 0.17 for teaching rooms, which gives 0.57 s for 200 m³. In the UK, BB93 lays down its own limits per room type and BS 8233 covers sound in buildings in general. Have the exact requirement for your room worked out by the architect or acoustician: the volume and the use set the figure. What no standard does is tell you which material to use; that remains the school's choice.

## How to measure reverberation time (and what you miss if you don't)

A reverberation measurement to ISO 3382-2 takes half an hour per room: a short impulse or noise, a microphone at a few positions, and a reading per octave band from 125 to 4,000 Hz. What it tells you that an estimate cannot:

- **Per frequency**: many rooms ring mainly in the low frequencies, where thin panels do little. Then you choose thicker panels or an air cavity.
- **Empty versus furnished**: the standard assesses the furnished room. Cupboards, desks and curtains count, but less than one hopes.
- **Before and after**: a measurement before the works and one after is the proof for the school board and, in grant applications, for the funding body.

Re-Sound measures on request; for a first estimate the dimensions of the room are enough.

## Worked example: from 1.2 to 0.6 seconds

Classroom of 8 × 7 m and 3.2 m high (179 m³): tiled floor, plasterboard ceiling and a glazed wall. Measured: **1.2 s**. Target: **0.6 s**.

Sabine's formula (A = 0.161 × V / T):

- absorption present: 0.161 × 179 ÷ 1.2 = **24 m² Sabine**
- absorption at the target: 0.161 × 179 ÷ 0.6 = **48 m² Sabine**
- to add: **24 m² Sabine**

Proposal: **30 m² of absorbent ceiling** in class A (αw 0.9), good for 27 m² Sabine, plus **6 m² of [rWood Groove](/en/products/rwood-groove)** on the back wall (αw 0.90, 5.4 m² Sabine). Together well over 32 m² Sabine: comfortably above the 24 needed, so the room stays under 0.6 s even when empty and there is margin for the low frequencies. How to do the sum yourself is in our guide on [how many acoustic panels you need](/en/blog/how-many-acoustic-panels-do-i-need).

**Which reverberation time is required in a classroom?**

**Are wall panels alone enough in a classroom?**
